Audio transcript: Welcome: what our mods change

Welcome. This is the first of a series of short listens about how our Skyrim differs from the Skyrim you remember. This one is the map: what is installed, what that does and does not promise, and which episodes to listen to next.

First, what counts. Everything on this list is installed in our game. Installed means a mod is present and switched on. It does not mean every feature has been tried with a full party. At the time of recording, the group has not yet played this exact setup together. So whenever you hear the words "not yet tested in multiplayer", that is the honest state of things today. Revision seven of the collection is live on Nexus.

Here is what is installed, grouped by job.

For multiplayer and support, there is Skyrim Together Reborn, now at version one point eight point three, the Skyrim Script Extender, and Address Library. Skyrim Together is the one that turns a single-player game into a shared world. The other two work quietly behind it.

For combat and movement, there are Mortal Enemies, Precision, and True Directional Movement. They change how enemies swing, how your weapon connects, and how your character moves and locks on.

For magic, perks and races, there are Mysticism, Better Vanilla Perks, and Imperious. Mysticism reworks the spells. Better Vanilla Perks reworks what each perk gives you. Two small perk patches from the Friend Kit change some of those perks. Imperious replaces what each race gets. In our game, High Elf, Wood Elf, Dark Elf and Redguard have no racial power, and episode five explains it.

For the start of the game, there is Helgen Express. It swaps the long cart ride for a quick start screen.

For menus and settings, there are SkyUI, Skyrim Souls, MCM Helper and Display Tweaks. SkyUI gives you sortable inventory and magic menus. Skyrim Souls is the one to remember: most menus no longer pause the world.

For information on screen, there are TrueHUD, which puts health bars over enemies, and moreHUD, which tells you more about the things you look at.

For looting and interaction, there are QuickLoot and Better Third Person Selection. Both make picking things up faster. QuickLoot also needs a small helper called PapyrusUtil.

For the map, there is Atlas Map Markers, which adds hundreds of missing markers. Alongside it, Disable Fast Travel switches off fast travel by clicking the map. You get there on foot, or by carriage or ferry. Carriages and ferries should still work, but that has not been tested yet.

And three more change the feel of the world. Random Dragons No More switches off the random dragon attacks. Quest dragons stay. Cathedral Weathers and Seasons brings moodier weather, fog, thunder and darker nights. And Sounds of Skyrim Complete, with a small patch so it works alongside the weather, adds ambient sound in the wild, in caves, in ruins and at night.

For hunger and thirst, there is Simplest Survival, with Keyword Item Distributor to mark which items count as food and drink, and a small patch of our own that sets the timing.

For jobs and danger, there is The Notice Board, which puts job boards outside inns, and Vampire Attacks Restored, which brings back vampire attacks at night. A small patch of our own keeps those attacks away until level twelve.

For the world itself, there is Elysium Estate, a group house southwest of Whiterun, and it is required. How you get inside it has not been tested. Skyrim Trading Posts adds seven trading posts along the roads, with merchants and hired guards. Skyrim Bandit Expansion adds fourteen new bandit camps and two occupied mines. Skyshards hides one hundred and eight shards across the nine holds. Every three you absorb give one perk point, and each player collects their own. None of these three has been tested with two players yet. And Harborwatch is an optional house in Solitude.

Last, three optional mods add gear. They are Playable Greybeard Robes, Wizard Hats, and a Lord of the Rings weapon collection. Each player chooses whether to install them. If you skip one, you will not see that gear on players who have it, and they will not see yours.

Every feature in this series comes from the mods in these groups and our own small patches. There is no perk overhaul other than Better Vanilla Perks, and no spell pack other than Mysticism. If a video or a website shows something we do not have, it does not apply to our game.

Now, which episodes are for you.

Episodes one to five are for everyone. This is episode one. Episode two is joining and leaving: setting up once, starting offline, connecting, and leaving properly. Episode three is playing as a party: the leader, saves, going down, shared experience, what is and is not shared, and the features we are holding back until they are tested. Episode four is fighting in our Skyrim. Episode five is races and your racial power, which matters most before you create a character.

After those five, pick the path that matches how you want to play. If you will fight with blades, shields and armor, episode six is yours. If you will cast, episode seven covers attack and control, and episode eight covers healing and summoning. Healers and summoners can start with eight. If you will sneak, shoot a bow, pick locks or talk your way through, episode nine is the stealth path.

Episodes ten to twelve help everyone, but they can wait until after your first night. Ten is inventory, looting and a home. Eleven is builds off the beaten path. Twelve is what to go looking for.

You do not have to listen to everything. The first five give you enough to join in and stay out of trouble, and the rest are there when you want them.

That is the end of episode one.

Audio transcript: Joining and leaving

This is episode two, for everyone: how to get into the host's game, how to get through a night, and how to leave without losing progress.

Start with the one-time setup.

First, your game has to match. Revision seven of the collection is live. Follow the Nexus collection page for the installer choices. Revision seven also needs two perk patches from the Friend Kit, and everyone needs both before a shared session. Start the game from your Skyrim Together shortcut rather than the ordinary Skyrim launcher. If the game ever tells you the server has a different version of Skyrim Together, don't fiddle. Tell the host.

Second, Tailscale. This is how you reach the host's computer. Tailscale is an app that makes a private, encrypted connection between devices, and a free personal account is enough. The host shares one computer with you. Once you accept, your computer can reach that one computer and nothing else of theirs. It only goes one way: the host's computer can answer yours, but it cannot start connections to your devices. Your normal internet traffic does not go through Tailscale or through the host. You do not open ports or change any router settings.

Install it from Tailscale's own website. Windows asks once for permission. Check that the publisher says Tailscale Inc., then say yes. A small Tailscale icon appears near the clock. Right-click it and log in with a personal Google, Microsoft, Apple or GitHub account, not a work or school one. If it asks whether this is for business or personal use, pick personal.

Then open the share link the host sends you privately, and accept it with the same account you just used. Treat that link like a password. Now right-click the Tailscale icon, open Network devices, and you should see the host's computer listed. Unused links stop working after thirty days, so if the page says the invitation is no longer valid, ask the host for a new one.

Next, the offline start. Our start of the game is Helgen Express, which replaces the long cart ride and the execution with a start screen. You click through tabs to choose your race, your starting standing stone, your armor, your weapon, your spells and who helps you escape, and then you click Ready. Do all of that offline, before you connect, and make a save once you are out of the introduction. Choose your race with care, because episode five is about what each race really gets.

Now, joining. Check that Tailscale is running, start the game from your Skyrim Together shortcut, press Right Control or F two, and click Connect. You will see an Address box and a Password box. The host sends you the address and the password privately, along with exactly what to type, so nothing is read out here. Type them in and click Connect. You should hear a confirmation and see the words "Successfully connected to server" in the small chat window.

If instead you see "Could not connect to the specified server", check three things. Is Tailscale running? It stops when you sign out of Windows or restart. Did you sign in with the same account that accepted the share? If not, right-click the icon, choose the account that accepted it, or add it. And did the share go through? If the host's computer is not in Network devices, open the link again. A Windows note saying there is no internet access on the Tailscale network is harmless.

Now, during the night. The leader launches the party. As the server is set, everyone who connects joins it automatically. Also as the server is set, one game day lasts about seventy-two real minutes. The server runs the clock, so nobody can sleep or wait time forward. Your save lives on your own computer, so save at safe moments, not only at the end. Episode three covers the rest of party life.

Last, leaving. It is three steps: save, disconnect, then quit. Let the save finish. Then press F two, choose Disconnect, and then Proceed. After that, use Quit to Main Menu, which works properly under Skyrim Together. If you go on to exit the program itself, expect it to crash at the very end. That happens on the Skyrim Together versions we have run. It happens after your save is written, and it is harmless apart from leaving a crash file behind. No fix is proven, but there is no need to worry about it.

If you have to leave in a hurry, the steps are the same, just quicker. Save if you can, let it finish, then disconnect and quit. If you have a moment, step somewhere safe first.

If you want Tailscale off between nights, right-click its icon and choose disconnect. Just remember to check it is running before the next one.

That is the end of episode two.

Audio transcript: Playing as a party

This is episode three, for everyone. It is about what changes when several people share one Skyrim: who leads, what is shared, what stays yours, and what falling down means now. Episode four takes the fighting itself.

Start with the shared world. In ordinary Skyrim the world exists for you alone. It waits while you read, and it reloads when you die. Under Skyrim Together, other players' characters travel the same roads and fight the same enemies as you. Enemies, their equipment, and the contents of chests are shared between our games.

Quests come first. One player is the party leader, and for our campaign it stays the same person the whole way through. When the leader's quest moves forward, everyone else follows the leader together. Only the leader should talk to quest characters and pick up quest items. Everyone can talk a choice over, but the leader makes it in the dialogue. Everyone else should keep their hands off quest actions, because in the version we run, the server does not check who made a quest change. If someone starts a quest by accident, that is fine, as long as they do not advance it before the leader has started it too. As the server is set, miscellaneous quests are not shared at all. Anyone who does one does it alone. The Notice Board puts job boards outside inns, and most of its jobs are the miscellaneous kind. So the leader takes a job and everyone goes along, but the others may not see it in their journals. Who gets the reward has not been tested.

What about quest rewards? We do not know yet. Who receives the gold or items a quest hands out has not been tested, and it may depend on whether the reward comes from a quest step or from a conversation. We will watch the first hand-in. And Skyrim gives no experience for finishing a quest anyway. You level by using skills.

If the leader's game drops, our reading of the code says leadership passes to whoever is next on the list. So agree beforehand who would take over.

Now saving. Each player keeps their own saves on their own computer. The server does not store your character, so save before the session ends. Use one character per campaign. If some of you want a solo game or a smaller group game, make a separate character, so the group characters do not move ahead while someone is away.

Next, going down. As the server is set, the death system is on, and it takes no gold. When you fall, the screen fades, and about five seconds later you get back up at full health. Outdoors that is nearby, and inside a dungeon it is the entrance. Then you cannot be hurt for about ten seconds. You lose no items, and there is no reload. Dying may also pay off your bounties, but that has not been tested here. If it does, it is handy by accident and a loophole on purpose, so agree not to use it. As for helping a fallen friend, we found no way for a healer to stand someone back up. That is our reading of the code, not something we have watched.

As the server is set, experience is shared for combat. When anyone gains experience in a combat skill, everyone else gains the same amount in whichever combat skill they last used. The combat skills here are One-handed, Two-handed, Archery, Block, and the schools of Alteration, Conjuration, Destruction, Illusion and Restoration. Nothing else is shared. Smithing, alchemy and enchanting level only for whoever is doing the crafting, and the armor, stealth and speech skills level only for their own user. Whether every skill gains exactly as it would alone, especially when you heal a friend, is not yet tested in multiplayer.

Difficulty. As the server is set, everyone plays on Master, and Skyrim Together enforces the server's difficulty on every player while connected. Master is harder than the default setting. It does not add enemies or strengthen them because more players joined. Four players hitting one enemy end the fight faster, so a party shares the danger, while someone tanking alone feels it most. That is reasoning, not something we have watched.

As the server is set, friendly fire is off: player damage between us is switched off. Followers, guards and townspeople are not players, though. They can still be hit, and they will still take offence.

Now loot, gold and supplies. Gold is personal. Everyone has their own purse and nothing is pooled. Chests and bodies in the world are one shared pile, so as far as we can tell, loot goes to whoever grabs it, not to everyone at once. Nobody has watched that in our exact setup yet, so agree who takes what. Whether you share one shopkeeper's gold, or each see your own, has not been confirmed. As the server is set, dropped items are not shared. Chests in the city homes you can buy, and in Elysium Estate, are private to each player. Hearthfire houses are unclear: the wiki says personal, but the game files suggest shared, and nobody has tested it. There is also no trading window. So handing something over means a chest outside a home. It is shared only while everyone is in the same area. No hand-over chest has been tested, so try a cheap item first. Episode ten goes into homes and handing things over. Crafted potions and enchanted gear may not reach another player. Other players report this, and it has not been tested here, so craft for yourself. Everyone drinks from their own pack, so a party burns a party's worth of potions, and on Master you will probably drink more. Our setup does add hunger and thirst, from Simplest Survival. About thirty real minutes after you last ate, Hunger sets in, and about thirty after you last drank, Thirst does. Each one cuts your health, magicka and stamina regeneration in half. Eating food clears Hunger, and drinking a potion clears Thirst. The clock starts the first time you eat or drink. Nothing in our setup adds cold.

Chat and short calls help when nobody can pause. Typing in the small chat window works, so nobody needs a microphone. A few calls are worth agreeing. "Ready" means you are ready to go. "Hold" means find safety and wait. "Help" means help me now, and say where you are. "B R B" means you are taking a break. "On me" means an enemy is chasing you and you are coming back toward the group.

Finally, here are the features the group is holding back until they have been tested together. Later episodes mention only the ones in their own skills.

Two perk patches from the Friend Kit change some perks. Perks the patches changed are not on this list. They are fine to take, but nobody has played them together yet. From Mysticism, we hold Mark and Recall, and everything that raises a corpse, including those scrolls and staves. From Better Vanilla Perks, we hold Dark Souls two, the Conjuration perk that can make raised bodies explode. We allow only one summon per player. From Imperious, we hold Contingency, Beast Tongue, Spirit Walk and Red Sand Dance. Held means do not take or use it until the host announces that its group test passed.

That is the end of episode three.

Audio transcript: Fighting in our Skyrim

This is episode four, for everyone: what a fight feels like in our Skyrim. Episode three explained that the server is set to Master, and that is the backdrop for everything here.

Start with the enemies, because you will notice them first. In ordinary Skyrim, an enemy who starts a swing can keep turning while the attack plays out. You step to the side and the axe follows you round. Mortal Enemies changes that. Enemies commit to a swing once it starts, and they cannot spin all the way around in the middle of it. So the wind-up is worth watching. You see a draugr raise its sword, you step aside, the blow lands where you were, and now you have a moment to hit back. Sidestep and punish becomes a real pattern. It does not make enemies weaker, and on Master a committed blow that lands still hurts. Whether the timing looks the same on every player's screen is not yet tested in multiplayer, so learn it together on the first night.

Next, how you move. In ordinary Skyrim, third person movement is tied tightly to the camera. With True Directional Movement, your character turns and runs in whatever direction you press, while the camera stays free. You can run toward the camera, circle an enemy and back away naturally.

It also adds target lock. Press the middle mouse button to lock onto an enemy. A marker appears on the target, and your character keeps facing it. Move the mouse to switch to another enemy, and press the middle button again to let go. If you use True Directional Movement, keep its hostile-only lock-on setting on. Then you cannot lock onto a friend, so nobody fixes their camera on a teammate in a chaotic fight. It also means a healer cannot use lock-on to aim a heal. Healing a friend means aiming by hand.

Then, health bars, from TrueHUD. It puts health bars over the enemies in the fight, adds a large boss bar for important opponents, and shows target information when you lock on. In a group that is quietly useful, because everyone can see which enemy is nearly finished. Whether TrueHUD reliably shows the health of other players is not yet tested in multiplayer, so do not count on it to tell you a friend needs healing. Listen for them instead.

Now the rhythm of a fight. Skyrim Souls keeps most menus live, so they do not pause the game. If you open your bag in the middle of a fight, an enemy can still hit you, and your friends are not frozen by your menus. Step back to a safe spot before you browse. Put your potions and spells in your favourites before you go into a dungeon.

Your own weapon is changed by Precision, which decides hits by where the blade or hammer actually travels. A wide sideways swing can catch several enemies in one pass, and a thrust that misses on screen misses in the game. Episode six covers that in depth for melee players, but two points matter to everyone. The first is spacing. Big weapons sweep a real arc, so a frontliner needs room, and friends should not stand right beside a swing. As the server is set, player damage is off, so you will not hurt each other, but the swing still has to travel somewhere. The second is the people who are not players. Followers, guards and townspeople can still be struck, and hitting one still makes them angry, which is easier to do by accident in a crowded room. Older reports from other players say hit reactions can look different on another person's screen. How that looks across our connected games is not yet tested in multiplayer.

One thing to know about kill cams. Kill cams may appear when you are not connected. When you are connected, they are off.

Now some changes that are new in our game, and they all touch fights.

First, dragons. In ordinary Skyrim, once the story gets going, random dragon attacks can happen as you travel. Random Dragons No More switches those off. Quest dragons stay. We checked the mod's file, and its off switch is locked, so the game's own quest should not turn random attacks back on. Skyrim Together's own documentation says some random encounters may not appear in everyone's world, which is a good reason not to have them. The mod is still being tested, so we have not yet watched a play session with it.

Second, the weather. Cathedral Weathers and Seasons brings moodier skies, fog, thunder and darker nights. Expect it to be harder to see what is coming. Weather in Skyrim Together is decided by the party leader's game, so everyone should see the same sky. It is not yet tested in multiplayer with our group.

Third, the sound. Sounds of Skyrim Complete adds ambient sound in the wild, in caves, in ruins and at night. In ruins and caves you may hear trouble before you see it. The sounds play on your own computer, so nothing needs to match between players.

Fourth, vampires. Vampire Attacks Restored brings back vampire attacks on towns and villages at night, outside the walled cities. A small patch of our own keeps them away until level twelve. Until level thirty they are less likely. Random encounters may not appear for everyone, so how an attack looks for each player is not yet tested in multiplayer.

Fifth, bandits. Skyrim Bandit Expansion adds fourteen new bandit camps and two occupied mines across Skyrim. Go together. This is not tested with two players.

So, put together. Enemies commit to their swings, so you can dodge them. You move freely and lock onto the dangerous enemy, never a friend. Health bars show which target is nearly done. Menus do not pause, so prepare before the dungeon. Random dragons are meant to be off, vampires may attack towns at night, there are new bandit camps, and the world is darker and louder. And when you fall, as episode three said, you get back up and rejoin your friends.

That is the end of episode four.

Audio transcript: Races and your racial power

This is episode five of our series on what our mods change. It is for anyone choosing a character, and it finishes the group of episodes that everyone should hear.

Start with the ordinary game. Every race begins with a few passive advantages and one power you can use from the first minute. Nords resist frost. Bretons resist magic. Khajiit have claws and night vision.

Imperious is the mod that changes this, and it replaces that package rather than adding to it. Each race gets a new set of passive abilities, and most of them come with real tradeoffs. Each race also has a power that you earn by reaching a racial goal during play, instead of having it from the start. The familiar bonus does not simply stay on top. Nords are the clearest example. In the ordinary game they resist half of frost damage. Here they resist a quarter, and they gain other things, such as hitting harder as they get badly hurt.

You choose your race on the Helgen Express start screen, before you connect. Your powers then appear in the Magic menu, under Powers. Any race can learn any skill, so a Wood Elf can be a healer and an Orc can be a mage. Race gives you flavor and a few edges. It does not lock you into a class. And the characters of each race out in the world get these changes too, not only players.

One more thing before the tour. The mod also reshuffles starting health, magicka, stamina and carrying capacity, so check your race's numbers on the character screen.

Now the ten races, with what each one gains and what each one loses.

High Elves become enchanters. Their enchantments are fifteen percent stronger, but their weapons and armor are fifteen percent weaker. They recover magicka and stamina faster when either one drops below half, at some cost to their defenses. A High Elf makes a good mage or spellblade, but an early pure caster feels the lost defense.

Argonians swim twice as fast and keep waterbreathing. They also recover from the brink, getting resources back when they run very low. Their earned power is Caustic Spit, a strong spit that weakens an enemy's armor and magic resistance, and you earn it by finding four underwater chests. They lose the ordinary Argonian healing power and the disease resistance. This race suits explorers and mobile support.

Wood Elves become hunters. Their Harrier ability marks prey, and they can sense movement while standing still. They can also eat from corpses to gain attributes. They lose their ordinary animal-command power and their resistance to poison and disease. They make natural scouts.

Bretons lose some magic resistance. It drops from twenty-five percent to fifteen, and Dragonskin is gone. In exchange they gain extra effects from standing stones, and spell absorption that switches on when their magicka runs low. After you discover ten standing stones, the Breton ability called Shared Ancestry lets a Breton borrow a human or elf's racial power for five minutes. That sounds wonderful, but it comes with a catch that we will get to in a moment.

Dark Elves draw on their ancestors. They can spend favor to call on a guardian that gives them extra armor for two minutes, and they gain favor from marked kills. They resist fire by twenty-five percent instead of fifty. The ordinary fire cloak is gone.

Imperials get an aura that gives nearby allies bonuses to armor and attack. They also recover some combat resource faster, and they make a choice at levels ten, twenty and thirty. After twenty quests they earn a refill of magicka and stamina. They lose the ordinary power that calms a crowd. An Imperial suits a party face or a guardian.

Khajiit move fifteen percent faster, and they sneak more quietly, with a stamina-powered leap called Prowl. Their claws deal ten damage, and power attacks add damage that scales with stamina. The ordinary night vision is gone until you earn its replacement by collecting fifty thousand gold over your lifetime.

Nords, as we said, resist frost by a quarter. They choose favored enemy races at levels ten, twenty and thirty. After clearing ten dungeons they earn a half-minute of very fast magicka and stamina recovery, and during it their frost resistance climbs to three quarters. They lose Battle Cry.

Orcs get a burst of speed and resources at the start of a fight, and a jump that staggers enemies once per battle. Their weapons and armor are twenty percent better, and their enchantments are twenty percent worse. After staggering enemies with that jump a hundred and fifty times, they earn Berserk, which doubles attack and critical damage and halves spell costs for fifteen seconds. The ordinary minute of Berserker Rage is gone, and Berserk does not give back its protection.

Redguards build momentum from humanoid kills, with a short boost to damage, critical hits and costs. They never run out of stamina outside combat, and they recover faster as their health falls. Imperious takes away their ordinary Adrenaline Rush power and their poison resistance, though they gain disease resistance in its place. More on racial powers in a moment.

Now the part that matters most, which is the powers.

As episode three explained, some features are held. Held means we are not using the feature until it has been tested with a group. Four of the earned racial powers are held. Contingency for High Elves, which casts stored spells for you. Red Sand Dance for Redguards, which slows time. Beast Tongue for Wood Elves, which recruits an animal companion. And Spirit Walk for Dark Elves, which summons ancestors. That is also the catch with Shared Ancestry. A Breton cannot borrow a held power, so those four stay off limits there too.

Imperious removed the original power of every race, and for four races the replacement is held. That leaves High Elves, Redguards, Wood Elves and Dark Elves with no racial power in our game.

So if you pick a High Elf, a Wood Elf, a Dark Elf or a Redguard, you keep the passive abilities, but you will not use a racial power.

As episode one said, installed does not mean tested together. Several racial effects have not been tested in multiplayer at all. We do not yet know whether the Imperial aura reaches other players, or how Prowl and Harrier behave for friends.

So choose for the story you want. A High Elf can be a mage. A Khajiit or a Wood Elf makes a scout. A Nord or an Orc fits a fighter, an Argonian an explorer, and an Imperial the one who speaks for the group. Race helps, but you can learn every skill.

The races each have goals that unlock their power, and we cover those in a later episode. The next episodes follow the three paths, starting with the fighter's.

Audio transcript: The combat path: blades, shields and armor

This is episode six of our series on what our mods change. It is for the melee fighter, and it covers One-handed, Two-handed, Block, Heavy Armor and Smithing. If you have heard episodes one to five, you are ready for it. As episode five said, Orcs and Nords suit this path, though any race can learn any skill.

Two mods shape a fight for you, and episode four introduced them. Here is how they land on a fighter. As episode four said, Mortal Enemies makes enemies commit to their swings. For you, that commitment is the opening. You watch the wind-up, you step aside or raise your shield, and the blow arrives where you used to be. Then you hit back.

The second reminder is about difficulty. As episode three said, the server is set to Master, which is harder than the default setting. That is why the opening matters so much, and why a fighter who simply trades blows will not enjoy the night. A party fighting together shares the damage, while a lone fighter has to absorb all of it to win the same fight. That is reasoning, not something we have watched happen. Fight together, and keep your health up.

Now Precision, which is the biggest change to your own weapon, so it deserves some time.

In the ordinary game, a melee hit lands if the enemy is roughly in front of you and roughly in reach, whatever the blade did on screen. Precision follows the weapon itself. The hit is decided by where the blade or the hammer actually travels. Three things matter, and they are reach, angle and spacing.

Reach first. The length of your weapon counts. A long weapon touches things a short one cannot, and a thrust that misses on screen misses in the game. Angle second. A wide sideways swing can catch several enemies in one pass, but a swing from a bad angle can slide right past a target that looked close enough. Spacing third. A good fighter keeps enemies at the distance where the weapon works best, and keeps friends out of the arc.

There is also a little feedback when you connect. You get a very short pause on impact and a small camera shake, so a solid hit feels solid. That is the mod telling you the blow landed.

Spacing has a second side, which is friends. As the server is set, player damage between friends is switched off, so you cannot hurt each other. But the swing still has to go somewhere. That follows from how Skyrim Together shares containers, and nobody has watched it happen in our exact setup, so the honest advice is this: agree before you start who takes what, or accept that quick fingers win.

The Skyrim Together wiki says the contents of chests are synced, and several players can look at the same chest and take from it. As the server is set, things dropped on the ground are not shared, and the Skyrim Together wiki says another player picking one up does not sync either. So never use the floor to give something to a friend.

Vendors need a word. In ordinary Skyrim, shopkeepers start with a limited amount of gold, and it refreshes over time. Whether four players share one vendor's gold, or each see their own, has not been confirmed. If it is shared, four people's loot will empty a shop fast. Blacksmiths, jewelers and general-goods vendors will buy ore and ingots, and ordinary vendors will not buy stolen goods unless you have the Fence perk. Skyrim Trading Posts adds seven trading posts along the roads, with merchants and hired guards. Trading there has not been tested with several players. Also remember that no one in Skyrim Together can sleep or wait time forward, so you cannot wait out a restock.

Next, your quest rewards. As episode three said, the party leader's game runs the important conversations. The leader's quest progress is copied to everyone, though the other players may not see a job-board job in their journals. Who receives a reward, whether gold or an item, has not been tested in multiplayer. It is a thing to watch at the first hand-in.

Now handing things over.

There is no built-in trade window. The route that should work is a chest out in the world, not in a house. No hand-over chest has been tested. A chest outside a home is shared only while everyone is in the same area. When the last player leaves, the server forgets what is in it, and the next player in can overwrite it. So use it only while all of you are present. One player puts the item in, closes the chest, and the other takes it out. Try one plain item first, then ten gold.

Two more routes are less certain. The wiki lists pickpocketing another player as something that syncs, but a report from twenty twenty-two said it stopped working after an update, and nobody has confirmed how it behaves now. And finished potions and custom enchantments are the risky items: one report says a player-made potion could not be shared, and another said a custom enchantment arrived as the plain item. The wiki says enchanted and poisoned items sync, and the reports disagree with each other, so treat all of it as not yet tested in multiplayer. Crafted potions and enchanted gear may not reach another player. Other players report it, and it has not been tested here. So the safe habit is to craft for yourself. Gear from the optional robes, hats and Lord of the Rings weapon mods is another case. A friend without those mods will not see it, and handing it over is untested.

Now homes.

As the server is set, the chests in the city homes you can buy, such as Breezehome, are not synced. What one player puts in a house chest is private to that player, so another player will not see it. That makes a house a good place for your own things, and a bad place to leave a delivery for a friend. Hearthfire houses are unclear. The wiki says their chests are personal, the game files suggest they are shared, and nobody has tested it. Test your own chest with cheap items first. Use a chest in the open world for hand-overs, and only while everyone is present. Our game also has two group houses. Elysium Estate, southwest of Whiterun, is required, and Harborwatch, in Solitude, is optional. Chests inside Elysium Estate are private to each player. For Harborwatch, that comes from its files and our server settings, and it has not been tested in the game. We have not tested how you get into either house.

Finally, buying a house. In ordinary Skyrim, you meet the city's requirements, then pay the steward. The price for Breezehome is five thousand gold. How buying a house works for four people at once has not been tested in multiplayer, so decide as a group who is buying and what you will do with it before anyone spends the gold. The leader handles the prerequisite quests, and another player's purchase may not buy you a home.

One last habit. Each player keeps their own saves on their own computer. The server does not store your character for you, so save before you quit.

So, put together. Most of your menus do not pause, so get ready before the fight. Looting is quick, but the pile is shared, so agree who takes what. Your gold is yours. Hand things over through a world chest while everyone is there, never the floor, and never a house chest. Try cheap items first. And keep your crafted goods for yourself until the group has tested them.

That is the end of this episode.

Character ideas that struggle alone but shine with friends, and why a group changes the math on Master difficulty.

Audio transcript: Builds off the beaten path

This episode is for everyone who is still deciding who to be. It is about characters that might struggle if they played alone, but come into their own with friends. It makes sense after episodes one to five. Every idea here is a suggestion, not a class. Any race can learn any skill, and you can mix these freely.

Start with why a group changes the math.

As the server is set, the difficulty is Master, which is harder than the default setting. That is the same for everyone, and enemies do not get stronger because more players have joined. Here is some simple reasoning, not something anyone has watched happen. Four players all hitting one monster add their damage together, so the fight ends faster. But one person alone on Master has a much harder time, and a character who deals little damage has the hardest time of all.

That is the opening for characters built around other people. A healer, a controller, a support caster and a talker are all weaker alone than they are in a party, and in a party they can be the reason everyone is still standing. A word of honesty about every build here: anything that depends on another player receiving your effect has not yet been tested in multiplayer.

Now, what the three big mods open up.

Mysticism reworks spells in every school and adds many new ones. Heal Other becomes an Apprentice spell, so a healer can look after friends from early on. Paralysis moves from Alteration into Illusion. Better Vanilla Perks keeps the shape of the perk trees but rewrites what the perks do, so familiar names may do new things. Imperious replaces each race's package with a different one, as episode five explained. Together they let you build characters that the ordinary game never really supported.

Here are the builds.

The healer. The idea is to keep everyone standing. The skills are Restoration first, then Alteration or one armor type, and a cheap way to fight back. A Breton or an Imperial suits it, but any race works. Mysticism gives a healer Heal Other, then Close Greater Wounds at Adept level, and then area heals called Breath of Life and Stendarr's Mercy. Grand Healing is channelled in Mysticism, not a single burst, so hold the cast. Better Vanilla Perks adds magicka regeneration to the Restoration mastery perks, which suits long fights.

How to play it. Stand behind the frontline and slightly to one side. Aim heals by hand, because lock-on cannot select a friend. Keep enough magicka for one rescue heal. A few cautions. The perk called Resurgence only lowers your own spell cost. In our game it makes Restoration spells cost ten percent less magicka, and it is allowed. Necromage, the Restoration 70 perk, is locked by our perk patch, so skip it. And healing other players goes through a path that Skyrim Together supports, but our exact spells, amounts and experience gain have not yet been tested in multiplayer. Also, there is no resurrection spell that a healer can cast on a fallen friend. As the server is set, a fallen player respawns nearby, or at the entrance if you are indoors, without losing gold. How that feels in our party has not been tested in multiplayer.

The paladin. The idea is a healer who can also hold the line. The skills are One Handed, Block and Restoration. Mysticism's Sunbeam, and the Sun Fire spell from Dawnguard, give light-based attacks against the undead. As episode four said, Mortal Enemies makes enemies commit to their swings, so a shield fighter can read the wind-up. For a paladin, that commitment is the moment to cast. Casting a heal means taking a hand off your shield, so call "cover my cast" and let a friend step in.

The necromancer-healer. The idea is to keep the living alive while a conjured helper holds the enemy off. The skills are Restoration first, Conjuration second, and one defense. Mysticism adds Conjure Skeleton and Conjure Skeleton Archer, and familiar and atronach spells fill the early helper role. These are conjured creatures, not corpses raised from the floor. Place the helper to one side and leave the escape lane clear. Budget your magicka for both the summon and a rescue heal. And let the summon expire rather than spend your healing reserve to recast it. The group's budget is one controlled summon per player, and raising dead bodies is held. Skip the Necromage perk, because our perk patch locks it. Episode three lists the held features.

The death knight. A heavy fighter with a dark helper. The skills are Two Handed and Heavy Armor, with a little Conjuration. A Nord or Orc fits well. Summon before you commit, then fight with your weapon. Frost magic or a health-draining enchantment adds flavor. This build does not heal the group, so someone else needs to cover that job.

The grey wizard. The idea is a travelling spellcaster with damage, protection and backup healing. The skills are Destruction and Alteration, then some Restoration. A Breton or a High Elf fits the theme. Bretons lose some reliable magic defense. A High Elf gets Highborn from a Friend Kit race patch: faster magicka regeneration for a minute, once a day. That is tested alone, not yet with two players. Mysticism's Destruction adds health-stealing spells such as Vampiric Touch, and Alteration adds Corrode Armor, which weakens an enemy before a friend hits it. Carry a second damage type for enemies that resist fire. A ward protects you, but whether it shields a friend is not yet tested in multiplayer.

The controller. The idea is to change what enemies can do instead of hitting the hardest. The skills are Illusion and Alteration, plus one reliable attack. Any race works. Mysticism expands control spells and weakening effects, and the morale spells Courage, Rally and Call to Arms have a route in Skyrim Together's code that reaches other players, though their effect in our party is not yet tested in multiplayer. Call "leave that one" when you have an enemy under control, so your friends do not break it. And keep an answer ready for enemies that resist control.

The storyteller. The traveller who talks, trades and carries the story. The skills are Speech, Restoration and one weapon. An Imperial, a Breton or a Khajiit fits well. Better Vanilla Perks gives Speech more than prices, as episode nine explained. There is no music or bard spell system in our build, and the quest leader still speaks in shared quest dialogue. So this is a flavor of play, more than a toolkit.

The Redguard spellsword. A fast fighter who keeps moving. The skills are One Handed, Light Armor and Destruction. Redguards gain momentum from humanoid kills and never run out of stamina outside combat. A Friend Kit race patch gives Redguards Adrenaline Rush back: faster stamina regeneration for a minute, once a day. That is tested alone, not yet with two players. Red Sand Dance is held.

The artisan. This one may surprise you. In our party, only combat experience is shared. Alchemy, Smithing and Enchanting level only for the player who does the crafting, so one person doing all the crafting levels the craft fastest. Better Vanilla Perks gives every material its own smithing perk, an Arcane Blacksmith perk for improving magical gear, strong Enchanter ranks, and the alchemy perks. A High Elf is a natural enchanter, with stronger enchantments at the cost of weaker physical gear. An Orc goes the other way. The risk is the hand-over. Passing finished custom potions and enchanted gear to a friend has not been tested in multiplayer, so craft for yourself first and share raw materials.

Last, the pairings.

A guardian and a healer benefit from predictable positioning. The guardian holds a corner, and the healer stands behind it. A guardian and a ranger divide the target jobs: one holds the melee while the other removes the archer or caster. A paladin and a wizard need explicit healing cover, so agree on who heals while the paladin is busy blocking. A necromancer-healer with a frontline needs clear summon placement, so the helper takes one flank and the exit stays empty.

One reminder for every build. Read perk and spell descriptions, but do not treat them as measured results. Stendarr's Aura says ten damage, while its underlying damage spell stores eight, and the real damage is unverified. Race bonuses come with losses too. The best character is the one you want to play for a whole campaign.

That is the end of this episode.

Audio transcript: What to go looking for

This is a short episode for everyone, and it can wait until after your first night. It makes sense after episodes one to five. It is a shopping list of things in our game that are worth hunting for, because they are new, or because they work differently from the Skyrim you remember.

Start with spells.

Mysticism changes what is inside every school of magic. By one count, nearly three hundred spells are taught by tomes, and a handful of those are held, which is covered in episode three. Here are some to look out for. In Restoration, Heal Other comes at Apprentice level, then Close Greater Wounds at Adept, then Breath of Life and Stendarr's Mercy. There are also three circles you place on the ground, called Circle of Life, Circle of Wisdom and Circle of Strength. They restore health, magicka or stamina around a spot, and whether they reach other players is not yet tested in multiplayer. In Destruction, look for Vampiric Touch and Vampiric Bolt, which steal health. In Alteration, look for Feather and Corrode Armor. In Illusion, look for Paralysis, which has moved there, and the morale spells Courage, Rally and Call to Arms. In Conjuration, look for Conjure Skeleton and Conjure Skeleton Archer.

Where do you buy them? Spell tomes teach them. Which vendor stocks which spell has not been confirmed, and the fact that a tome exists does not prove that a vendor sells it. So when you walk into a shop, read the list.

Next, racial goals.

As episode five explained, Imperious gives each race a power that you earn by meeting a goal, instead of having it from the first minute. Six races have goals that lead to a power the group permits. Argonians earn Caustic Spit, a strong debuff on enemy armor and magic resistance, by finding four underwater chests. Bretons earn Shared Ancestry by discovering ten standing stones, and it lets them borrow a human or elf's racial power for five minutes, though the group's holds still apply to whatever they borrow. Imperials earn Colovian Star after twenty quests. Khajiit earn Perception, longer night vision, after collecting fifty thousand gold over their lifetime. Nords earn Avatar after clearing ten dungeons. Orcs earn Berserk after one hundred and fifty Shockwave staggers. Whether progress toward these goals shows up properly for every player has not been tested in multiplayer, so keep your own count.

A reminder for the other four races. Friend Kit race patches give High Elves Highborn and Redguards Adrenaline Rush back, and give Wood Elves a new power, Hunter's Mark, each once a day. The first two are tested alone, not yet with two players, and Hunter's Mark is not tested in the game yet. Dark Elves are the only race still without a power.

Now perks worth reading again.

Better Vanilla Perks keeps the eighteen trees, but rewrites what many perks do. A few that are worth finding. Armsman, the first One Handed perk, now adds critical chance as well as damage. In Restoration, the mastery perks add magicka regeneration. The perk Resurgence makes Restoration spells cost ten percent less magicka. It only lowers your own spell cost. Heavy and Light Armor set bonuses work with three matching pieces instead of four. In Speech, Dragonborn replaces Haggling. Rank two gives fifteen percent better prices and adds no extra summon, and ranks four and five give twenty-five and thirty percent better prices. In Alchemy, Green Thumb gives you two ingredients from each plant. In Conjuration, Summoner and Puppeteer let you cast summoning spells twice and three times as far away, but they add no extra summon. And a Nord can buy Intimidation to get Battlecry, though that costs a perk and is not the original free power. Always read the description before you spend a point, and remember that a description is not a measured result.

Now gear, if you like dressing the part. Three optional mods add craftable gear. Playable Greybeard Robes adds a robe, hood and boots, and Wizard Hats adds hats in many colors. A Lord of the Rings weapon collection adds twenty-six craftable weapons. Ask who has them, because a friend without them will not see the gear, and they will not see yours. Handing gear to a friend without the mod has not been tested.

Now the map.

Atlas Map Markers adds hundreds of extra map markers. It is optional, and if you skip it your map shows only the usual markers. You can turn categories on and off in Mod Configuration. When you are looking for something to go and find, open the map first. Skyrim Souls keeps most menus live, so nothing pauses for the party while you look. Get out of danger first. But you cannot fast travel by clicking the map, because Disable Fast Travel switches that off. You walk, or take a carriage or a ferry, which should still work but have not been tested.

Last, what revision seven adds to the world. Revision seven is the live collection. None of it has been tested with several players yet.

Cathedral Weathers and Seasons makes the weather moodier, with fog, thunder and darker nights. The mod's page describes a power called Cathedral Meditation, where you can adjust how dark the nights are. Weather is shared across the party, and the party leader's game decides it.

Sounds of Skyrim Complete adds ambient sound in the wild, in caves, in ruins and at night, with its Cathedral patch so the two fit together. Sound is played on each player's own computer, so there is nothing to match between players.

Random Dragons No More switches off the random dragon attacks. Quest dragons stay. So in our game, a dragon should be somewhere you go to find, not something that jumps you on the road.

The Notice Board puts job boards outside inns. The leader takes a job and everyone goes along, though the others may not see the job in their journals.

Vampire Attacks Restored brings vampire attacks to towns and villages at night, outside the walled cities. None happen before level twelve, and they are less likely until level thirty. So be ready when you walk into a village after dark.

Simplest Survival adds hunger and thirst. About thirty real minutes after you last ate or drank, your health, magicka and stamina regeneration drops to half, until you eat food or drink a potion. So bring both.

Skyrim Trading Posts adds seven trading posts along the roads. Merchants buy and sell all kinds of items, and hired guards protect them. With map fast travel off, they give you a reason to walk the roads. Under our load order, guards or players may walk through some buildings of the Eastmarch post, near Caravan's End Tower. That pathing is untested.

Skyrim Bandit Expansion adds fourteen new bandit camps and two occupied mines across Skyrim. One mine is a frozen lair. The other is a gold and iron mine held by an Orcish gang. Go together.

Skyshards hides one hundred and eight Skyshards across the nine holds. Every three you absorb give one perk point. Each player collects their own.

Our game also has two group houses. Elysium Estate, southwest of Whiterun, is required. Harborwatch, in Solitude, is optional. Chests inside Elysium Estate are private to each player. For Harborwatch, that comes from its files and our server settings, and it has not been tested in the game. We have not tested how you get into either house.

And one more thing to try. At your first quest hand-in, write down each player's gold, items and skills before and after. Nobody has tested who receives quest rewards in multiplayer, and your own notes will settle it faster than any guide.
